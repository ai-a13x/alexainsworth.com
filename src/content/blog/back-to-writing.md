I used to write a lot. Then I became the first PM at Cleo AI, took eighteen months off to travel and spent a year of it teaching scuba diving in Honduras, came back and spent two very full years helping Runna grow from around 100k subscribers to over a million. Somewhere in there, the writing stopped.

I want to start again, for two reasons.

The first is that the product job is changing faster than at any point in my career. The gap between having an idea and having something working in front of users has collapsed. I now run most of my week on a personal AI operating system I built myself, and the questions that matter have shifted from "can we build this?" to "should we, and how will we know it worked?". I have a lot of opinions about what that means for PMs. Some of them will turn out to be wrong, and writing is the fastest way I know to find out which.

The second is that I've had a lot of help over the years from people who wrote down what they'd learned. It feels like time to return the favour.

## What I'll write about

- **The changing PM job.** What AI changes about product management, and what it doesn't.
- **Product in startups.** The chaos, the leverage, and the things nobody warns you about when you're the first PM, or the tenth.
- **Good PMs and good managers.** What I've learned from building and running product teams, including the mistakes.
- **Building with AI.** The operating systems, loops and small tools I build to make the job easier, with enough detail that you can steal them.
- **Builder culture.** Why I think every PM should be shipping something of their own.

## How this will work

I'll aim for one longer post a month here, with shorter thoughts on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/alex-ainsworth-product/) in between. If you'd like to follow along, there's an [RSS feed](../../rss.xml), or just check back now and then.

If something here is useful, tell me. If you think I've got it wrong, definitely tell me. That's the whole point.
